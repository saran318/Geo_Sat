## Section 1: Remote Sensing & Satellite Imagery Fundamentals

### Q1: What is the Sentinel-2 satellite mission, and what are its key characteristics?
**Answer:** Sentinel-2 is a wide-swath, high-resolution, multi-spectral imaging mission developed by the European Space Agency (ESA) under the Copernicus program. It comprises two twin satellites (Sentinel-2A and Sentinel-2B) operating in the same sun-synchronous orbit, providing a 5-day revisit time. It acquires 13 spectral bands spanning VNIR to SWIR at spatial resolutions of 10m, 20m, and 60m.

### Q2: Which 4 raw spectral bands are extracted from Sentinel-2 in this project, and what are their spatial resolutions?
**Answer:**
1. **Band 2 (Blue - 490 nm)**: 10m resolution. Useful for atmosphere/water body mapping.
2. **Band 3 (Green - 560 nm)**: 10m resolution. Sensitive to vegetation greenness and water turbidity.
3. **Band 4 (Red - 665 nm)**: 10m resolution. Absorbed by chlorophyll in green plants.
4. **Band 8 (NIR / Near-Infrared - 842 nm)**: 10m resolution. Strongly reflected by plant leaf cellular structure.

### Q3: What is the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), how is it calculated, and what do its values signify?
**Answer:** NDVI measures vegetation density and health.
$$\text{NDVI} = \frac{\text{NIR} - \text{Red}}{\text{NIR} + \text{Red} + 1e-6} = \frac{\text{B8} - \text{B4}}{\text{B8} + \text{B4} + 1e-6}$$
Values range from -1.0 to +1.0:
- **-1 to 0**: Water bodies, snow, or clouds.
- **0 to 0.2**: Bare soil, rock, or urban built-up areas.
- **0.2 to 0.5**: Sparse vegetation, pasture, or shrubland.
- **0.6 to 1.0**: Dense forest or healthy crops at peak growth.

### Q4: What is the Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI), and why is it included?
**Answer:** NDWI delineates open water features and enhances water body boundaries.
$$\text{NDWI} = \frac{\text{Green} - \text{NIR}}{\text{Green} + \text{NIR} + 1e-6} = \frac{\text{B3} - \text{B8}}{\text{B3} + \text{B8} + 1e-6}$$
Water features exhibit positive NDWI values because water strongly reflects green light while absorbing near-infrared light.

### Q5: What is the Sentinel-2 Scene Classification Layer (SCL)?
**Answer:** SCL is a pixel-level classification band generated by the Sen2Cor atmospheric correction processor. It categorizes each pixel into surface or atmospheric types, such as 3 (Cloud Shadows), 8 (Cloud Medium Probability), 9 (Cloud High Probability), 10 (Thin Cirrus), and 11 (Snow/Ice), enabling automated cloud masking.

### Q6: Why is digital reflectance normalized by dividing by 10,000?
**Answer:** Sentinel-2 L2A Top-Of-Atmosphere (TOA) and Bottom-Of-Atmosphere (BOA) reflectance values are stored as unsigned 16-bit integers (UINT16) scaled by a factor of 10,000. Dividing by 10,000.0 converts integer Digital Numbers (DN) back into true physical reflectance values in the standard range $[0.0, 1.0]$.

### Q7: What spatial resolution is used in this project?
**Answer:** All 4 primary bands (B2, B3, B4, B8) naturally operate at 10 meters per pixel spatial resolution. Consequently, a 64x64 pixel patch represents a $640\text{m} \times 640\text{m}$ ground area ($40.96$ Hectares).

### Q8: What is EuroSAT?
**Answer:** EuroSAT is a benchmark multi-spectral satellite imagery dataset consisting of 27,000 georeferenced $64\times 64$ patches extracted from Sentinel-2 across 10 European land use categories.

### Q9: What are the 10 Land Use and Land Cover (LULC) classes in the EuroSAT dataset?
**Answer:**
1. AnnualCrop, 2. Forest, 3. HerbaceousVegetation, 4. Highway, 5. Industrial,
6. Pasture, 7. PermanentCrop, 8. Residential, 9. River, 10. SeaLake.

### Q10: What is the difference between Spatial Reference System (SRS) and Coordinate Reference System (CRS)?
**Answer:** SRS and CRS are synonymous terms describing how 2D pixel coordinates $(x,y)$ on a raster map correspond to actual geographic locations on Earth (e.g. UTM Zone 32N EPSG:32632 or WGS84 EPSG:4326).

## Section 2: Deep Learning & ResNet50 Architecture

### Q11: Why is ResNet50 preferred over a custom CNN from scratch?
**Answer:** ResNet50 features residual skip connections ($\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$) that resolve the vanishing gradient problem in deep networks. Pretrained weights on ImageNet transfer rich low-level edge, texture, and pattern representations, accelerating convergence and improving generalization.

### Q12: Standard ResNet50 accepts 3 input channels (RGB). How was it modified to accept 6 channels?
**Answer:** The first convolutional layer `model.conv1` was replaced:
- Original: `Conv2d(3, 64, kernel_size=7, stride=2, padding=3)`
- New: `Conv2d(6, 64, kernel_size=7, stride=2, padding=3)`

### Q13: How were weights initialized for the 3 newly added channels (NIR, NDVI, NDWI)?
**Answer:** To preserve pretrained filter dynamics:
- The first 3 channel weights were copied directly from the original RGB weights.
- The 3 new channel weights were initialized using the mean of the pretrained RGB weights across the channel dimension (`original_conv1.weight.mean(dim=1, keepdim=True)`).

### Q14: Which layers of ResNet50 were frozen during fine-tuning?
**Answer:** Early feature extraction blocks (`conv1`, `layer1`, `layer2`) were frozen by setting `requires_grad = False`. Only the newly modified `conv1`, deep residual blocks (`layer3`, `layer4`), and the final fully-connected classifier `fc` were trained.

### Q15: What is the input shape and output shape of our ResNet50 model?
**Answer:**
- **Input Tensor Shape**: `[Batch_Size, 6, 64, 64]` (Channels: Blue, Green, Red, NIR, NDVI, NDWI).
- **Output Tensor Shape**: `[Batch_Size, 10]` (Raw unnormalized class logits).

### Q16: Why is Softmax applied to the model outputs during inference?
**Answer:** Softmax converts raw real-valued output logits $z_i$ into a normalized probability distribution $P(y=i)$ where $\sum_{i=1}^{10} P(y=i) = 1.0$:
$$P(y=i) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{10} e^{z_j}}$$

### Q17: What loss function and optimizer were used during training?
**Answer:**
- **Loss Function**: Cross-Entropy Loss (`nn.CrossEntropyLoss()`).
- **Optimizer**: AdamW (`weight_decay=1e-4`, `learning_rate=1e-4`).

### Q18: What learning rate scheduler was applied?
**Answer:** Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler (`CosineAnnealingLR`), which smoothly decays the learning rate following a cosine curve down to a minimum threshold.

### Q19: What is Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP)?
**Answer:** AMP (`torch.cuda.amp.autocast()`) executes matrix multiplications in half-precision floating points (`float16`) while keeping critical accumulation steps in single-precision (`float32`). It cuts GPU memory usage by ~50% and speeds up training without losing accuracy.

### Q20: What early stopping strategy was implemented?
**Answer:** Training monitors Validation Loss after every epoch. If validation loss fails to improve for 5 consecutive epochs, training halts early to prevent overfitting, and the best model weights are restored from `resnet50_best.pt`.

## Section 3: Data Preprocessing & Memory Optimization

### Q21: What is windowed reading in `rasterio`, and why is it critical?
**Answer:** Satellite tiles are massive (often $>10,000 \times 10,000$ pixels, $>1\text{ GB}$ per band). `rasterio.windows.Window(col_off, row_off, width, height)` loads only the specific $64\times 64$ patch needed into RAM, preventing Out-Of-Memory (OOM) crashes.

### Q22: How does Python's `yield` generator optimize memory during patch extraction?
**Answer:** `extract_patches()` uses `yield` instead of storing patches in a Python list. It yields one preprocessed $64\times 64\times 6$ patch at a time to the caller, immediately triggering garbage collection (`gc.collect()`) after consumption.

### Q23: How does `validate_bands()` verify satellite image integrity?
**Answer:** It opens each band file with `rasterio` and verifies that all bands share identical:
1. Coordinate Reference System (`crs`)
2. Affine Transform (`transform`)
3. Width and Height dimensions (`width`, `height`)

### Q24: What dataset splitting strategy was used?
**Answer:** A reproducible 70% Train, 15% Validation, 15% Test random split using `torch.utils.data.random_split` with a fixed seed (`manual_seed(42)`).

### Q25: Why were spatial data augmentations chosen over color jittering?
**Answer:** Color jittering alters brightness and contrast, distorting physical reflectance values and rendering NDVI/NDWI calculations invalid. Spatial augmentations (random horizontal/vertical flips, 15° rotations) preserve physical band ratios while building invariance to orientation.

## Section 4: Spatial Tiling, Stitching, & Georeferencing

### Q26: What is spatial stitching in remote sensing inference?
**Answer:** Spatial stitching recombines patch-level model predictions extracted via a sliding window back into a continuous 2D spatial raster grid covering the original satellite tile extent.

### Q27: How are overlapping patches handled during tile stitching?
**Answer:** `TileStitcher` uses accumulator arrays (`class_counts` and `confidence_sum`). For overlapping sliding windows (e.g. stride 32 on a 64x64 window), probability vectors or votes are summed per pixel. `np.argmax` resolves final land cover class, and confidence is averaged across overlapping passes.

### Q28: What spatial metadata must be preserved when exporting a prediction GeoTIFF?
**Answer:**
1. **CRS**: Coordinate Reference System (e.g. EPSG:32632).
2. **Transform**: 6-parameter Affine Transform matrix mapping pixel $(x,y)$ to georeferenced coordinates $(E, N)$.
3. **Dimensions**: Height and Width pixels.
4. **Nodata value**: Mask integer for unmapped areas (255).

### Q29: What is an Affine Transform matrix in `rasterio`?
**Answer:** An affine matrix maps pixel coordinates $(c, r)$ to spatial coordinates $(x, y)$:
$$x = a \cdot c + b \cdot r + c_{\text{off}}, \quad y = d \cdot c + e \cdot r + f_{\text{off}}$$
Where $a$ is pixel width, $e$ is negative pixel height, and $(c_{\text{off}}, f_{\text{off}})$ is top-left origin coordinate.

### Q30: How are prediction GeoTIFFs converted to WGS84 (EPSG:4326) bounding boxes for web maps?
**Answer:** Using `rasterio.warp.transform_bounds(src_crs, 'EPSG:4326', left, bottom, right, top)` to convert projected coordinates (meters) into geographic decimal degrees `[min_lon, min_lat, max_lon, max_lat]`.

## Section 5: Change Detection & Area Statistics

### Q31: What is post-classification change detection?
**Answer:** Post-classification change detection independently classifies two annual satellite images into land cover maps, then compares them pixel-by-pixel to identify transitions (e.g. Forest to Built-up).

### Q32: How does the system handle two annual GeoTIFFs with different dimensions or CRS?
**Answer:** `align_and_load_rasters()` checks spatial properties. If a mismatch is detected, it uses `rasterio.warp.reproject()` with `Resampling.nearest` to reproject and resample Year 2's raster onto Year 1's exact grid.

### Q33: How is a pixel-wise transition code calculated?
**Answer:** A unique integer code is assigned to each pixel:
$$\text{Transition Code} = (\text{Class}_{\text{Year1}} \times 100) + \text{Class}_{\text{Year2}}$$
For example, Class 1 (Forest) transitioning to Class 7 (Residential) produces code `107`.

### Q34: How is land cover area calculated in Hectares (ha)?
**Answer:**
$$\text{Pixel Area (m}^2) = |a| \times |e|$$
$$\text{Area (ha)} = \frac{\text{Pixel Count} \times \text{Pixel Area (m}^2)}{10,000}$$
At 10m spatial resolution, 1 pixel = $10\text{m} \times 10\text{m} = 100\text{m}^2$. Thus, 100 pixels = 1 Hectare.

### Q35: How is net percentage change calculated for a land cover class?
**Answer:**
$$\text{Percentage Change (\%)} = \frac{\text{Area}_{\text{Year2}} - \text{Area}_{\text{Year1}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{Year1}}} \times 100$$

### Q36: What output files are generated by the Change Detection module?
**Answer:**
1. `change_YYYY_YYYY.tif`: 2-band GeoTIFF (Band 1: Binary Change, Band 2: Transition Code).
2. `area_stats.csv`: Tabular CSV detailing pixel counts, area in ha/km², net change, and percentage shift.

## Section 6: Web Backend, FastAPI, & API Design

### Q37: Why was FastAPI chosen for the backend framework?
**Answer:** FastAPI provides high asynchronous performance (built on Starlette and Pydantic), automatic OpenAPI/Swagger documentation generation (`/docs`), strict request/response data validation, and native Python type hinting support.

### Q38: How does FastAPI's `lifespan` context manager optimize model inference?
**Answer:** Loading the 270MB ResNet50 model takes 2-3 seconds. The `lifespan` manager pre-loads the model weights into RAM/GPU once when the server boots up, caching it globally so endpoint requests execute in sub-50ms.

### Q39: What is CORS, and why is `CORSMiddleware` configured?
**Answer:** Cross-Origin Resource Sharing (CORS) is a browser security mechanism that blocks web pages from making API requests to a different domain or port. Adding `CORSMiddleware` with `allow_origins=["*"]` allows Person 3's frontend (e.g. running on localhost:3000) to communicate with the FastAPI backend (localhost:8000).

### Q40: What HTTP status codes are returned by our API?
**Answer:**
- `200 OK`: Request succeeded.
- `400 Bad Request`: Invalid file format or missing band channels.
- `422 Unprocessable Entity`: File upload is corrupted or unparseable by Rasterio.
- `503 Service Unavailable`: Model weights fail to load.
- `500 Internal Server Error`: Server processing failure.

### Q41: How does the API stream file uploads without filling server memory?
**Answer:** Incoming files are written to a temporary disk location via `tempfile.NamedTemporaryFile()` using `shutil.copyfileobj()`, processed, and immediately unlinked in a `finally` block.

### Q42: What is the function of `torch.inference_mode()`?
**Answer:** `torch.inference_mode()` is an upgraded context manager over `torch.no_grad()`. It completely disables autograd tracking, version counters, and tensor view tracking, yielding lower memory consumption and faster execution.

### Q43: How does automatic model downloading work if `resnet50_best.pt` is missing?
**Answer:** `utils.download_model_weights_if_missing()` checks if the checkpoint file exists locally. If missing, it streams the binary file from GitHub Releases (`requests.get(url, stream=True)`) into `models/resnet50_best.pt`.

### Q44: What endpoints are exposed in `api.py`?
**Answer:**
1. `GET /health`: Health status & device info.
2. `POST /predict`: Single patch classification.
3. `POST /classify-tile`: Full tile sliding-window classification & GeoTIFF stitching.
4. `POST /change-detection`: Multi-year change map generation & CSV area stats.

### Q45: How does Person 3's frontend submit files to `/predict`?
**Answer:** As a `POST` request with `Content-Type: multipart/form-data`, passing the image file under the form key `"file"`.

## Section 7: Software Engineering & Project Integration

### Q46: How is modularity maintained across the 3 project team members?
**Answer:**
- **Person 1**: Preprocessing & Model Training (`src/preprocessing/preprocess.py`, `training/`).
- **Person 2**: Backend, Inference, Stitching, & Change Detection (`src/inference/`, `change_detection/`, `api.py`, `utils.py`).
- **Person 3**: Frontend User Interface.

### Q47: What design patterns were followed to avoid code duplication?
**Answer:** The DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself) principle. Common routines (logging, band scanning, array-to-tensor processing, WGS84 bound calculations) are centralized in `utils.py` and `backend_config.py`.

### Q48: How are unit and integration tests structured in `test_api.py`?
**Answer:** Uses `pytest` and `fastapi.testclient.TestClient`. It includes an `autouse` mock model fixture (`setup_mock_model`) that instantiates a dummy PyTorch module if weight files are absent, allowing CI/CD test passes.

### Q49: What is PEP 8 compliance, and how is it enforced?
**Answer:** PEP 8 is the official Python style guide. Enforced via 4-space indentation, snake_case function/variable names, PascalCase class names, upper-case constants, docstrings, type annotations, and explicit imports.

### Q50: How can this project be scaled in production?
**Answer:**
1. **Containerization**: Package the FastAPI backend using Docker.
2. **Asynchronous Task Queue**: Offload tile stitching and change detection to Celery with Redis/RabbitMQ.
3. **Cloud Storage**: Store prediction GeoTIFFs in AWS S3 or Google Cloud Storage.
4. **Tile Server**: Integrate `titiler` or `geoserver` to stream prediction GeoTIFFs directly to interactive frontend Leaflet/Mapbox maps as XYZ map tiles.
